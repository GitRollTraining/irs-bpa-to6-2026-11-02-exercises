# Answer: three changes that make bad replies fail

| Requirement | Change | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Only the amount may be null | In `schema/output.schema.json`, `"next_action": {"type": "string"}`, as on `main`. | The lab's notes always state a next action: note 0138 ends "Wait for the call.", so the null in reply 0138 is wrong. The fix goes in the schema, which the extractor also sends to the model. A field that your own data can leave out needs null too (section 3.1, below the table of fields). |
| No guess at an amount | In `extractor.py`, remove the six lines from `# The model sometimes`. The schema then fails replies 0104 and 0105. | A guess can be wrong: these lines turn "40,000 of 95,000" in reply 0105 into 4000095000. So a reply that fails counts as a failure and is never repaired. Even "18,400", one clear number, breaks the schema: the count then shows how often the model does that, and each new form of text would need a rule of its own (section 3.1, below the second figure). |
| Your own check of the tax year | `import datetime` above `import json`. Just above `return fields`, after the schema check: `if fields["tax_year"] > datetime.date.today().year:`, then `raise ExtractionFailed("the tax year is after this year")`. | The schema passes any integer, so only your own check can fail 2203, a year that has not begun. After the schema check, `tax_year` is an integer, so `>` can compare it with this year; before it, `tax_year` can be missing or text, and `>` stops with an error (section 3.1, the list above the second figure, and the second figure). |

The check also accepts any schema that lets only `amount_in_question` be null and keeps the rest of the contract; any `check_reply` that fails an amount as text and returns each reply that passes as it came; and any own check after the schema check that fails 2203 and passes 2020 to 2023, the years in `notes/`, such as a range from 2000 to this year, or `"minimum"` and `"maximum"` in the schema. Passing does not prove that a reply is right: this answer still passes tax year 2022 for a note that names 2023. A first year stops a typo such as 1023; only a look at the note stops 2022.
